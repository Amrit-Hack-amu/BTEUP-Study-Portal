1️⃣ Introduction to Security Standards

High priorityChapter weight: Theory

1.1 What is a Security Standard?

📘 Definition

A security standard is a documented set of agreed rules, requirements and best practices that tells an organization what level of security it must reach and which controls it should use to protect information.

Security standard ek likha hua, sabke dwara maana hua rulebook hota hai. Ye batata hai ki information ko surakshit rakhne ke liye kam se kam kaun-se rules aur controls follow karne hain.

🧠 Detailed explanation

A standard is made by experts and published by a recognised body such as ISO (International Organization for Standardization), IEC, NIST or BIS. An organization compares its own security with the standard. The difference is called a gap. It then closes the gap using controls (safeguards such as passwords, firewalls, backups, training).

Standard experts banate hain aur ISO, IEC, NIST, BIS jaisi sansthayen publish karti hain. Organization apni security ko standard se compare karta hai. Jo kami milti hai use gap kehte hain. Phir controls (password, firewall, backup, training) laga kar gap bharta hai.

🎯 Objectives
  • Give a common language and a common minimum level of security.
  • Reduce risk in a consistent way instead of random, personal choices.
  • Help to meet legal and customer requirements (compliance).
  • Allow measurement, audit and certification of security.
  • Build trust between customers, partners and the organization.
  • Sabke liye ek common bhasha aur minimum security level dena.
  • Risk ko ek jaisi tarah se kam karna – har kisi ki apni marzi nahi.
  • Kanoon aur customer ki zarurat (compliance) poori karna.
  • Security ko naapna, audit karna aur certificate dena.
  • Customer aur partners ka bharosa jeetna.
🏫 Real-life example

A college runs an online exam portal. If every teacher sets his own password rule, some will use “1234”. A standard says: “All accounts must use at least 8 characters, backups must be taken daily, and logs must be kept for 6 months.” Now everyone follows the same minimum level.

College ke online exam portal me agar har teacher apna password rule banaye to koi “1234” bhi rakh dega. Standard kehta hai: password kam se kam 8 character, roz backup, 6 mahine ke log. Ab sab ek hi minimum level follow karte hain.

⚙️ Practical application

Banks follow RBI cyber-security rules and PCI DSS for cards; hospitals protect patient records; IT companies get ISO 27001 certificates to win foreign clients; colleges follow university and AICTE/state IT rules for data and exam security.

Bank RBI ke rules aur card ke liye PCI DSS follow karte hain; hospital patient record bachate hain; IT company foreign client paane ke liye ISO 27001 certificate leti hai.

⭐ Advantages & importance

Fewer security incidents, easier audits, customer trust, legal safety, better business opportunities, and a clear roadmap for new staff.

Kam attacks, aasan audit, customer ka bharosa, kanooni suraksha aur naye staff ke liye clear roadmap.

✍️ Exam-ready answer (3–5 marks)

Security standard: A documented set of rules, requirements and best practices that defines the minimum security level and controls an organization must follow to protect its information.

Objectives: (1) common minimum security, (2) consistent risk reduction, (3) legal and customer compliance, (4) audit and certification, (5) trust. Example: ISO/IEC 27001, NIST CSF, PCI DSS.

Yaad rakho: Standard = “Kam se kam kya karna hai” ka likha hua rulebook.

1.2 Why do organizations need security standards?

  1. Legal compliance – laws and regulators expect “reasonable security”.
  2. Customer trust – a certificate proves security is checked by outsiders.
  3. Consistency – all branches and departments follow the same rules.
  4. Risk reduction – proven controls stop common attacks.
  5. Business advantage – tenders and foreign clients often ask for ISO 27001 or similar.
  6. Benchmarking – you can compare yourself with other organizations.
  1. Kanoon aur regulator “reasonable security” maangte hain.
  2. Certificate dikhane se customer ka bharosa badhta hai.
  3. Sab branches ek hi rules follow karti hain.
  4. Proven controls common attacks rokte hain.
  5. Tender aur foreign client ISO 27001 maangte hain.
  6. Dusre organizations se apni tulna (benchmark) kar sakte ho.

1.3 Security Standard vs Policy vs Procedure

Policy = what and why (high-level management rule). Standard = how much / which exactly (mandatory technical requirement). Procedure = how, step by step (detailed instructions). Guideline = suggestion (optional advice).

Policy = “kya aur kyun” (management ka bada rule). Standard = “kitna aur kaun-sa exactly” (zaroori technical requirement). Procedure = “kaise, step by step”. Guideline = sirf salah (optional).

Example (password): Policy – “Passwords must protect all accounts.” Standard – “Minimum 8 characters, one number, change every 90 days.” Procedure – “Step 1: open Settings → Step 2: choose Change Password …”. Guideline – “Use a passphrase of 3 random words.”

Types of standards
  • Mandatory vs voluntary: Laws and regulator rules (RBI) are mandatory; ISO 27001 is voluntary but often demanded by clients.
  • Management vs technical: ISO 27001 manages the whole system; a standard for encryption (e.g. AES) is technical.
  • International vs national vs industry: ISO (world), BIS (India), PCI DSS (card industry).
  • Mandatory = zaroori (kanoon/regulator), Voluntary = apni marzi (ISO 27001).
  • Management standard poora system chalata hai, technical standard ek technology (jaise AES) ke liye.
How an organization adopts a standard

Limitations: costs money and time; paperwork; a certificate does not guarantee zero attacks; needs regular review.

Limitation: kharcha aur samay lagta hai, paperwork badhta hai, certificate ka matlab “kabhi attack nahi” nahi hota.

🟣 ADVANCED / EXTRA KNOWLEDGE — Level 3 (Advanced)
🟣 LEVEL 3 — ADVANCED
  • Compliance ≠ security. A company can pass an audit and still be hacked. Mature organizations use standards for risk-based security, not just a tick-box.
  • Control mapping: One control (e.g. “backup”) maps to ISO 27001 Annex A, NIST CSF “Recover/Protect” and CIS Control 11. Many organizations build one control set and map it to all standards (a GRC – Governance, Risk, Compliance – approach).
  • Maturity models: Organizations rate themselves from level 1 (ad-hoc) to level 5 (optimised), e.g. CMMI-style, to plan improvement.
  • Certification ecosystem in India: Independent certification bodies are accredited by NABCB (under the Quality Council of India); the government agency STQC also offers ISMS certification.
  • Regulators: CERT-In (national incident response agency) requires reporting of cyber incidents within 6 hours under its 2022 directions; RBI, SEBI and IRDAI issue sector rules.
  • NIST CSF 2.0 (2024) added the Govern function, stressing leadership and supply-chain risk.

Sirf audit pass karna security nahi hai. Mature companies standards ko risk samajhne ke liye use karti hain, sirf tick-box ke liye nahi. Ek hi control ko ISO, NIST aur CIS teeno se map kiya ja sakta hai.

2.1 Introduction, definition, history and purpose

📘 Definition

ISO/IEC 27001 is the international standard that gives the requirements to establish, implement, maintain and continually improve an Information Security Management System (ISMS).

ISO/IEC 27001 ek international standard hai jo batata hai ki ISMS (Information Security Management System) ko kaise banana, chalana, maintain karna aur lagatar behtar banana hai.

🧠 Detailed explanation

An ISMS is a system of policies, people, processes and technology that manages information security risks in an organization. ISO 27001 does not say “buy this firewall”. It says “find your risks, choose suitable controls, check them, and keep improving”. It is a risk-based standard and it is certifiable – an outside certification body can audit you and issue a certificate.

ISMS ek system hai jisme policies, log, process aur technology milkar information security ke risk sambhalte hain. ISO 27001 ye nahi kehta ki “ye firewall kharido”. Ye kehta hai: apne risk dhundo, sahi controls chuno, unhe check karo aur lagatar sudhaar karo. Ye certificate wala standard hai.

📜 History & purpose – timeline
1995 – BS 7799 published by British Standards Institution (first information security code).
2000 – Adopted by ISO as ISO/IEC 17799.
2005 – ISO/IEC 27001:2005 (the certifiable ISMS requirements) published; 17799 later renamed ISO/IEC 27002.
2013 – ISO/IEC 27001:2013 (114 controls in 14 groups).
Oct 2022 – ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (93 controls in 4 themes). Current edition.
Feb 2024 – Amendment 1:2024 “Climate action changes” – organizations must decide whether climate change is a relevant issue (clause 4.1) and whether interested parties want climate-related requirements (clause 4.2).
31 Oct 2025 – End of the transition period: 2013-edition certificates are no longer valid.

Purpose: protect confidentiality, integrity and availability of information; manage risk; meet legal and customer needs; show trust.

🏢 Real-life example

A software company in Lucknow handles a foreign bank’s data. The bank asks: “Are you ISO 27001 certified?” After certification the company wins the contract.

Lucknow ki ek software company foreign bank ka data handle karti hai. Bank poochta hai “ISO 27001 certified ho?”. Certificate milne ke baad contract mil jata hai.

✍️ Exam-ready answer (Define ISO 27001)

ISO/IEC 27001 is an international standard that specifies the requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining and continually improving an Information Security Management System (ISMS). It is risk-based and certifiable. The current edition is ISO/IEC 27001:2022.

2.2 ISMS and the CIA Triad

ISMS exists to protect the CIA Triad: Confidentiality (only authorised access), Integrity (accurate and complete), Availability (usable when needed).

ISMS ka kaam CIA triad bachana hai: Confidentiality (gopniyata), Integrity (sahi aur poora data), Availability (zarurat par uplabdh).

  • Confidentiality breach example: exam paper leaked before the exam.
  • Integrity breach: someone changes attendance records.
  • Availability breach: ransomware locks the college server on result day.
  • Paper leak = Confidentiality toota.
  • Attendance badal di = Integrity toota.
  • Ransomware se server band = Availability toota.

Risk = chance that a threat will use a vulnerability and harm an asset. A simple formula: Risk = Likelihood × Impact.

2.6 Implementation process, internal audit, management review and certification

Implementation steps
Internal audit and management review
  • Internal audit (clause 9.2): done by the organization’s own trained, independent auditors to check that the ISMS follows the standard and its own rules. Must be done at planned intervals, before the certification audit.
  • Management review (clause 9.3): top management reviews audit results, incidents, risks, objectives and decides improvements and resources.
  • Internal audit = apne hi log (jo us department ke nahi hain) check karte hain.
  • Management review = top management meeting jisme sab results dekh kar faisle liye jate hain.

  1. Select an accredited certification body (in India, accredited by NABCB).

  4. Certificate issued (valid for 3 years) after non-conformities are fixed.
  5. Surveillance audits every year, then recertification after 3 years.

2.7 ISO 27001 vs ISO 27002 and practical example

ISO 27001 = requirements (“what you must do” – can be certified). ISO 27002 = guidance on the controls (“how to do it” – cannot be certified). Full comparison table: Difference Tables.

27001 = “kya karna zaroori hai” (certificate milta hai). 27002 = “controls kaise lagayen” (sirf guide, certificate nahi).

🏢 Practical example – “TechNova Solutions” (fictional IT company, 120 staff)
  1. Management decides: “Our client work in Noida must be ISO 27001 certified.” (Clause 5)
  2. Scope: development centre, servers and the HR/IT teams. (Clause 4)
  3. Risk assessment finds: laptops without disk encryption, shared passwords, no tested backups.
  4. Treatment: BitLocker encryption, password manager and MFA, daily backup with monthly restore tests.
  5. Training for all staff; visitor log and ID badges added.
  6. Internal audit finds 3 minor gaps; they are fixed. Management review is held.
  7. Stage 1 and Stage 2 audits are passed; certificate is issued; yearly surveillance starts.
✍️ Exam-ready answer (5 marks) – “Explain ISO 27001 implementation.”

Steps: (1) management commitment, (2) define ISMS scope, (3) risk assessment, (4) risk treatment plan and Statement of Applicability, (5) implement Annex A controls and train staff, (6) monitor and measure, (7) internal audit, (8) management review, (9) certification audit (Stage 1 and 2), (10) yearly surveillance and 3-year recertification.

3.1 Introduction, need and objectives

📘 Definition

The Information Technology Act, 2000 is the main Indian law that gives legal recognition to electronic records and digital/electronic signatures and defines cyber offences and penalties. It came into force on 17 October 2000.

IT Act, 2000 India ka mukhya cyber kanoon hai. Ye electronic record aur digital signature ko kanooni manyata deta hai aur cyber crime ki saza tay karta hai. Ye 17 October 2000 se lagu hua.

🧠 Why did India need cyber laws?
  • Old laws (IPC, Evidence Act) were written for paper – they did not accept e-mail or e-records as proof.
  • E-commerce and online banking were growing; contracts and signatures were needed in electronic form.
  • New crimes appeared – hacking, virus, online fraud – with no clear punishment.
  • The UN General Assembly recommended countries to adopt the UNCITRAL Model Law on Electronic Commerce (1996); India’s IT Act is based on it.
  • Government wanted e-governance (online forms, e-filing).
  • Purane kanoon kagaz ke liye bane the – e-mail ko saboot nahi maante the.
  • E-commerce aur online banking badh rahe the.
  • Hacking, virus, online fraud jaise naye crime ke liye saza nahi thi.
  • UN ke UNCITRAL Model Law (1996) ke aadhar par India ne ye Act banaya.

3.3 Electronic records, electronic signatures and digital signatures

📘 Definitions

Electronic record: data, record or image generated, sent, received or stored in electronic form.   • Section 5: legal recognition of electronic signatures.

⚙️ How a digital signature works

It gives: Authentication (who signed), Integrity (not changed), Non-repudiation (signer cannot deny).

✍️ Exam-ready answer – “Digital signature”

A digital signature is an electronic signature created by encrypting the hash of a message with the sender’s private key. The receiver verifies it with the sender’s public key. It provides authentication, integrity and non-repudiation. Certifying Authorities licensed by the CCA issue Digital Signature Certificates under the IT Act.

⚠️ HISTORICAL PROVISION – NOT CURRENT LAW

3.5 Section 66A – history and why it was struck down

What it was: Inserted by the 2008 amendment. It punished sending “grossly offensive”, “menacing” messages, or false information to cause “annoyance, inconvenience, insult, danger…” through a computer or communication device – up to 3 years and fine.

Problem: The words were vague. Police arrested people for ordinary Facebook posts and cartoons (for example the 2012 arrests of two young women in Palghar for a post about a bandh after a political leader’s death).

Judgment: On 24 March 2015 in Shreya Singhal v. Union of India, the Supreme Court of India struck down Section 66A in full as violating the freedom of speech under Article 19(1)(a) and not protected by Article 19(2). It also read down section 79(3)(b) – an intermediary must act only on a court order or government notification – and upheld section 69A (blocking) with safeguards.

Today: Section 66A cannot be used. Courts (2021–22) directed authorities to stop cases still being registered under it. Offences like threats or defamation are dealt with under other laws (now the BNS).

66A ek vague section tha jisme “offensive” message bhejne par saza hoti thi. Police ne iska galat istemal kiya. 24 March 2015 ko Supreme Court ne Shreya Singhal case me ise poori tarah radd kar diya. Ab ye kanoon nahi hai – exam me ise “current provision” ki tarah mat likhna.

✍️ Exam-ready answer

Section 66A of the IT Act punished sending offensive or menacing messages through communication services. It was declared unconstitutional and struck down in its entirety by the Supreme Court on 24 March 2015 in Shreya Singhal v. Union of India, as it violated Article 19(1)(a). It is therefore not in force.

4.1 Introduction, definition and importance

📘 Definition

Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) are legal rights given to a person or organization over the creations of the mind – inventions, writings, software, music, designs, logos and brand names – so that others cannot copy or use them without permission.

IPR ka matlab dimaag ki rachna par kanooni adhikar. Jis insaan ne invention, software, gaana, logo ya design banaya, uski ijazat ke bina koi use copy ya use nahi kar sakta.

🧠 Detailed explanation

Physical property (a house) can be locked. Ideas and software can be copied in seconds. IPR laws give the creator a limited-time monopoly so that he can earn from his work. After that period, the work becomes public.

Ghar par taala laga sakte hain, par software seconds me copy ho jata hai. IPR creator ko kuch samay ke liye sole adhikar deta hai taaki wo kamai kar sake.

⭐ Importance
  • Rewards creators and encourages innovation.
  • Protects business brand value and income.
  • Prevents piracy, counterfeiting and unfair copying.
  • Helps to sell, license or franchise the work.
  • Builds consumer trust (genuine products).
  • Creator ko inaam aur naye idea ko protsahan.
  • Brand aur kamai ki suraksha.
  • Piracy aur naqli maal ko rokna.
  • License ya bechne ka adhikar.
✍️ Exam-ready answer – “What is IPR? Why is it important?”

IPR are legal rights granted to creators over their creations of the mind such as inventions, literary and artistic works, software, designs and brand names. Main types: copyright, patent, trademark, industrial design, geographical indication and trade secrets. Importance: protects creators, encourages innovation, prevents piracy, protects business value and allows licensing.

4.2 Copyright

Copyright protects the original expression of ideas – books, articles, music, films, paintings, photographs and computer programs. It arises automatically when the work is created and fixed (registration is optional but helpful as proof). Idea itself is not protected – only the way it is expressed.

Copyright kisi idea ko nahi, balki idea ko likhne/banane ke tareeke ko bachata hai. Ye kaam banate hi apne-aap mil jata hai (registration optional, par saboot ke liye accha).

  • Law: Copyright Act, 1957 (amended 1983, 1994, 1999, 2012).
  • Term: literary, dramatic, musical and artistic works – author’s life + 60 years; films, sound recordings, photographs – 60 years from publication.
  • Rights: copy, publish, adapt/translate, perform in public, sell or rent. Moral rights of the author continue (right to be named, object to distortion).
  • Fair dealing (section 52): study, research, criticism, review, news reporting are allowed.

Avadhi: lekhak ki zindagi + 60 saal. Film, recording, photo ke liye 60 saal. Fair dealing me padhai, research, review ke liye limited use allowed hai.

Example: A YouTuber uses a film song as background music without licence → copyright infringement. A teacher shows a few lines of a book in class for teaching → fair dealing.

🟢 [BTEUP SYLLABUS TOPIC]

4.3 Patent

A patent is an exclusive right granted for a new invention (a product or process) for 20 years from the date of filing, in return for disclosing the invention to the public.

Patent kisi naye invention par 20 saal (filing date se) ka sole adhikar hai. Badle me inventor ko invention ki jaankari public karni padti hai.

  • Law: Patents Act, 1970 (major changes in 2005); controlled by the Patent Office (CGPDTM).
  • Conditions: novelty (new), inventive step (not obvious), industrial applicability (useful).
  • Software: “a computer program per se” is not patentable (section 3(k)), but an invention with a technical effect that uses software may be considered.

Example: A company invents a new fast-charging battery process and files a patent – no one else can make it in India for 20 years without a licence.

4.4 Trademark

A trademark is a word, logo, symbol, sound, shape or colour combination that identifies the goods or services of one business and distinguishes them from others.

Trademark wo naam, logo ya symbol hai jisse pata chale ki product kis company ka hai.

  • Law: Trade Marks Act, 1999. Registration is valid for 10 years and can be renewed again and again (every 10 years).
  • ™ = claims a mark (even before registration); ® = registered mark.
  • Infringement/“passing off” = using a similar mark to confuse customers.

Example: Using a logo very similar to a famous mobile brand on duplicate chargers = trademark infringement.

7.4 RTO, RPO, DR site types and recovery strategies

RTO – Recovery Time Objective: the maximum acceptable downtime – how quickly the system must be working again. RPO – Recovery Point Objective: the maximum acceptable data loss measured in time – how old the restored data may be.

RTO = system kitni der me wapas chalna chahiye (downtime). RPO = data kitna purana chalega, yaani kitne ghante ka data kho sakte hain.

Example: A bank says RPO = 5 minutes and RTO = 1 hour for its core banking. A college website may accept RPO = 24 hours and RTO = 8 hours. Smaller RTO/RPO = higher cost.

BCP keeps the whole business running (people, places, processes, IT). DRP is a part of BCP that recovers IT systems and data.

🔵 BASIC KNOWLEDGE — Level 1 (Basic)
🔵 LEVEL 1 — BASIC

Analogy: A wedding hall with a generator. If power fails, lights stay on, guests keep eating and the function continues. The generator is a continuity plan; repairing the main power line afterward is recovery.

Shaadi hall me generator ho to light jaane par bhi function chalta rehta hai. Wahi hai business continuity.

  • Disruption = anything that stops normal work.
  • Critical function = work that must not stop.
  • Resilience = ability to absorb a shock and keep going.
🔵 BASIC KNOWLEDGE — Level 2 (Intermediate)
🔵 LEVEL 2 — INTERMEDIATE
  • Key terms: MTD / MAO (Maximum Tolerable Downtime), RTO (must be shorter than MTD), RPO, minimum business continuity objective (MBCO).
  • Plan types under BCP: Crisis management plan, Emergency response plan, DRP, Communication plan, Pandemic plan, Cyber incident response plan.
  • Incident vs crisis vs disaster: incident (small, handled by routine), crisis (needs management decisions), disaster (long outage, needs DR/BCP).
  • Benefits: survival of the business, trust, fewer losses. Limitations: cost, time, needs regular updates and drills, can give false confidence if never tested.
🟣 ADVANCED / EXTRA KNOWLEDGE — Level 3 (Advanced)
🟣 LEVEL 3 — ADVANCED
  • ISO 22301 (Business Continuity Management Systems) is the certifiable international standard for BCM; it follows the same high-level structure as ISO 27001 so both can be integrated. Related: ISO 22313 (guidance), ISO/IEC 27031 (ICT readiness).
  • BCM professional practice: guidance from BCI (Business Continuity Institute) and DRI International; roles such as BC manager and crisis manager.
  • Supply-chain continuity: identify single points of failure, contract SLAs with RTOs, dual vendors, cloud-region diversity.
  • Cyber resilience: cyber incident response plan linked to BCP; ransomware scenarios; communication with regulators (CERT-In reporting within 6 hours).
  • Regulatory view in India: RBI, SEBI and IRDAI expect regulated entities to maintain board-approved BCP/DR policies, test them regularly and report outcomes.
  • Metrics: % critical processes covered by plans, test success rate, achieved vs target RTO, time to activate crisis team.

🧩 Real-Life Case Studies

These are illustrative scenarios (organization names are fictional) based on common real-world situations. They help you apply concepts in exam answers.
🟢 Case 1

College computer lab implements security policies

Situation: “Himalaya Polytechnic” has a 60-PC lab shared by 600 students. Everyone uses one admin password. Pen drives are freely used.

Security problem: A virus spreads through USB drives; a student deletes another batch’s project files; some PCs run cracked software.

Applicable concept: Security policies (AUP, password, access control), audit, backup.

Solution: The principal forms an IT committee. They draft an Acceptable Use Policy, give each user a unique login, remove the shared admin password, scan USBs, install licensed/open-source software, and take weekly backups. Students sign the policy; a lab audit is held every semester.

Lesson learned: A policy must be written, approved, communicated, enforced and reviewed – not just a notice on the wall.

Seekh: Policy sirf likhna kaafi nahi, usse approve, announce, lagu aur review bhi karna padta hai.

🟢 Case 2

IT company achieves ISO 27001 certification

Situation: “CodeBridge Pvt Ltd” (150 staff) wants to serve a European bank, which asks for ISO 27001 certification.

Security problem: No documented risk assessment, laptops not encrypted, no incident register, access rights never reviewed.

Applicable concept: ISO 27001 – ISMS, risk assessment, Annex A controls, internal audit, management review, certification audit.

Solution: Management defines scope and policy → risk assessment → treatment plan and SoA → disk encryption, MFA, access reviews, training → internal audit and management review → Stage 1 and Stage 2 audits → certificate (3 years) with yearly surveillance.

Lesson learned: ISO 27001 is about a continuous management system, not a one-time project. Top management support and evidence records are the key.

🟢 Case 3

A software developer faces copyright infringement

Situation: Riya, a freelance developer, creates a school-management app. A client copies the source code and sells it to other schools without paying.

Security / legal problem: Unauthorised copying and selling of software = copyright infringement / piracy.

Applicable concept: Software copyright (Copyright Act, 1957, ss. 63 and 63B), licensing, contracts.

Solution: Riya shows her work records, dated source-code history and the signed licence agreement. She sends a legal notice, asks the court for an injunction and damages, and may file a criminal complaint. In future she uses a clear licence, NDA and ownership clause, and registers the copyright.

Lesson learned: Define ownership and licence in writing before giving software to anyone. Keep proof of authorship (version history).

🟢 Case 4

Company hit by ransomware / server failure recovers its data

Situation: “Shree Pharma Distributors” discovers all files on the main server are encrypted by ransomware at 8 AM; invoices cannot be created.

Security problem: Loss of availability and possible data theft. Backups on the same network may also be encrypted.

Applicable concept: Disaster recovery – DRP, backup types, 3-2-1 rule, RTO/RPO, incident reporting.

Solution: The IT manager isolates the server, activates the DRP, rebuilds a clean server and restores from the offline daily backup (RPO: 24 hours). Accounts re-enter one day’s invoices. Passwords are changed, the infection path (a phishing e-mail) is closed, and the incident is reported to CERT-In and the police cyber cell.

Lesson learned: Keep an offline/off-site backup, test restores, train staff against phishing, and never depend only on paying a ransom.

🟢 Case 5

An organization maintains operations during a major disruption

Situation: A heavy cyclone cuts power and roads in the city where “Coastal Cooperative Bank” has its head office and data centre.

Security problem: Risk of long outage of branches, ATMs and online banking – availability and customer trust at stake.

Applicable concept: BCP – BIA, critical processes, emergency response, communication plan, DR site, alternate work arrangements.

Solution: BCP activated; staff safety first; core banking and ATM switch to the DR site in another city (RTO 2 hours); critical staff work remotely; branches in safe areas serve nearby customers; customers are informed by SMS and social media; regulator informed. After the cyclone, systems fail back and a review improves the plan.

Lesson learned: BCP covers people, places and processes, not only IT. Regular drills make the real response smooth.

Q1. What is a security standard? Why do organizations need it? (5 marks)

Definition: A security standard is a documented set of agreed rules and best practices that define the minimum security an organization must achieve and the controls to use.
Need: (1) Legal and regulatory compliance (2) Customer trust (3) Consistent security in all departments (4) Reduces risk with proven controls (5) Allows audit and certification (6) Business advantage in tenders.
Examples: ISO/IEC 27001, NIST CSF, CIS Controls, PCI DSS.

Q2. What is a security policy? Explain the policy development process. (5 marks)

Definition: A management-approved written statement of rules and responsibilities for protecting information and IT systems.
Process: 1) Identify need 2) Form team 3) Risk assessment and research 4) Draft 5) Review 6) Management approval 7) Communicate and train 8) Implement 9) Enforce and monitor 10) Review regularly.
Types: Organizational, issue-specific (AUP, password, e-mail), system-specific.

Q3. Explain ISO 27001 implementation steps. (5 marks)

ISO/IEC 27001 is the international standard for an ISMS. Steps: 1) Management commitment 2) Define ISMS scope 3) Information security policy 4) Risk assessment 5) Risk treatment plan and Statement of Applicability (Annex A controls) 6) Implement controls and train staff 7) Monitor and measure 8) Internal audit 9) Management review 10) Certification audit (Stage 1 and 2) 11) Surveillance audits yearly, recertification after 3 years.

Q4. Explain the security audit process with a flowchart. (5 marks)

Flowchart: Planning → Information gathering → Risk identification → Control evaluation → Audit execution → Analysis of findings → Audit report → Corrective actions → Follow-up.
Explain: Planning defines scope, objectives and team. Information gathering collects documents and interviews staff. Risks are identified and controls are tested with evidence. The report lists findings with risk ratings and recommendations. Corrective actions are assigned and the auditor follows up until closure.

Q5. Differentiate copyright, patent and trademark. (5 marks)

Copyright: protects original works (books, software, music); automatic; life + 60 years; Copyright Act 1957.
Patent: protects a new invention; must be granted by Patent Office; 20 years; Patents Act 1970.
Trademark: protects brand name/logo; registered; 10 years, renewable; Trade Marks Act 1999.
Examples: app source code – copyright; new battery process – patent; company logo – trademark.

Q6. Explain full, incremental and differential backups. (5 marks)

Full: copies all data; slowest backup, fastest restore, most storage.
Incremental: copies only changes since the last backup of any type; fastest backup, least storage, slowest restore (full + all incrementals).
Differential: copies changes since the last full backup; medium speed and storage; restore needs full + last differential.
Rule: follow the 3-2-1 rule: 3 copies, 2 media, 1 off-site.

Q7. Explain RTO, RPO and the types of DR sites. (5 marks)

RTO: maximum acceptable downtime (time to restore). RPO: maximum acceptable data loss measured in time (decides backup frequency).
Hot site: fully equipped, real-time data, recovery in minutes/hours, highest cost. Warm site: partial equipment, recovery in hours-days, medium cost. Cold site: empty space with utilities, recovery in days-weeks, lowest cost.

Q8. Differentiate between BCP and DRP. (5 marks)

(1) BCP keeps the whole business running; DRP restores IT systems and data. (2) BCP covers people, processes, premises, suppliers and IT; DRP covers technology only. (3) BCP is wider and DRP is a part of BCP. (4) BCP is used before, during and after a disruption; DRP mainly after an IT disaster. (5) BCP is led by management; DRP by the IT team. Example: BCP – staff work from home; DRP – restore database from backup.

Q9. What is software piracy? Explain its types and legal consequences. (5 marks)

Software piracy is the unauthorised copying, distribution or use of software. Types: soft-lifting, counterfeiting, internet piracy, hard-disk loading, client-server overuse. Law: Copyright Act, 1957 – section 63 (6 months to 3 years and ₹50,000–₹2 lakh) and section 63B for knowingly using an infringing computer program (7 days to 3 years and ₹50,000–₹2 lakh). Prevention: licensed or open-source software, audits, awareness.

Q10. Explain Business Impact Analysis (BIA). (5 marks)

BIA is the process of identifying critical business processes and measuring the impact (financial, legal, reputation, safety) if they stop. Steps: list processes → assess impact over time → identify dependencies → set MTD, RTO and RPO → rank recovery priority. Output: prioritised list used to build BCP and DRP. Example: in a bank, core banking/UPI is critical (RTO ~1 hour); training portal is low priority.

D. 📚 Long-answer questions (8–10 marks) with detailed model answers

Long Q1. Explain ISO 27001 in detail – ISMS, clauses, controls, risk assessment and certification.

Introduction: ISO/IEC 27001 is the international standard that specifies requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining and continually improving an ISMS. The current edition is ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (with Amendment 1:2024 on climate action changes). It is risk-based and certifiable.
ISMS and CIA: The ISMS is a system of policies, people, processes and technology that protects Confidentiality, Integrity and Availability.
Structure: Clauses 4–10: Context, Leadership, Planning, Support, Operation, Performance evaluation, Improvement + Annex A with 93 controls in four themes – Organizational (37), People (8), Physical (14), Technological (34).
Risk assessment: identify assets, threats, vulnerabilities → Risk = Likelihood × Impact → evaluate → treat (reduce, avoid, share, accept) → Risk Treatment Plan and Statement of Applicability.
Implementation: management support, scope, policy, risk assessment, controls, training, monitoring, internal audit, management review.
Certification: Stage 1 (documents), Stage 2 (implementation), certificate for 3 years, yearly surveillance, recertification.
Benefits: trust, compliance, fewer incidents. Limitations: cost, documentation, scope-limited, not a guarantee.
ISO 27001 vs 27002: 27001 = requirements (certifiable); 27002 = guidance on controls.
Conclusion: ISO 27001 gives a structured, continually improving way to manage information security.

Long Q2. Explain the Information Technology Act, 2000 – objectives, 2008 amendment, digital signature and important sections. What is the status of Section 66A?

Introduction: The IT Act, 2000 (in force 17 Oct 2000) is India’s main cyber law, based on the UNCITRAL Model Law on E-Commerce (1996).
Objectives: legal recognition of e-records and digital signatures; promote e-commerce and e-governance; define cyber crimes and penalties; set up CCA, adjudication system; amend IPC, Evidence Act.
2008 Amendment (in force 27 Oct 2009): electronic signature, new offences 66A–66F, 67A, 67B, section 43A, 69/69A/69B, 72A, revised section 79, CERT-In, investigation by Inspector-rank officers.
Digital signature: hash of message encrypted with private key; verified with public key; gives authentication, integrity, non-repudiation; certificates issued by licensed Certifying Authorities.
Sections: 43 – unauthorised access/damage, compensation; 43A – failure to protect sensitive data; 65 – tampering with source code (3 yrs/₹2 lakh); 66 – hacking (3 yrs/₹5 lakh); 66C – identity theft (3 yrs/₹1 lakh); 66D – cheating by personation (3 yrs/₹1 lakh); 67 – obscene content (3 yrs/₹5 lakh, repeat 5 yrs/₹10 lakh); 69 – interception and decryption; 70 – protected system (10 yrs); 72 – breach of confidentiality; 72A – disclosure in breach of contract; 79 – intermediary safe harbour.
Section 66A: punished sending offensive messages; struck down by the Supreme Court on 24 March 2015 (Shreya Singhal v. Union of India) for violating Article 19(1)(a). It is not law today.
Conclusion: IT Act gives legal base for e-commerce and punishes cyber crimes; data protection is now moving to the DPDP Act, 2023.

Long Q3. What is IPR? Explain its types, Indian laws and the differences between copyright, patent and trademark. Add a note on software piracy.

IPR: legal rights over creations of the mind. Importance: reward creators, encourage innovation, stop piracy.
Types and laws: Copyright (Copyright Act 1957); Patent (Patents Act 1970); Trademark (Trade Marks Act 1999); Industrial Design (Designs Act 2000); Geographical Indication (GI Act 1999); Trade secrets (contract and confidence law, no separate Act).
Differences: see Table 3 – subject, registration, term (life + 60 / 20 years / 10 years renewable), examples.
Software: a computer program is a literary work; the employer owns the work made in employment. Piracy: soft-lifting, counterfeiting, internet piracy, hard-disk loading. Penalty: sections 63 and 63B of the Copyright Act.
Open-source: MIT/BSD/Apache (permissive), GPL (copyleft).
Conclusion: Respecting IPR is both a legal duty and professional ethics for IT professionals.

Long Q4. Explain Disaster Recovery with the DR process, backup types, RTO/RPO and DR sites.

Definition: restoring IT systems and data after a disaster using a pre-planned strategy.
Disaster types: natural (flood, earthquake, fire) and human-made (ransomware, sabotage, human error, hardware/power failure).
DRP: documented plan – critical systems, RTO/RPO, team and call tree, backup details, alternate site, recovery steps, testing schedule.
Process diagram: Disaster → Detect and assess → Declare → Activate DR team → Failover → Restore data → Test and verify → Resume operations → Failback and review.
Backups: full, incremental, differential; on-site, off-site, cloud; 3-2-1 rule.
RTO/RPO: RTO = acceptable downtime; RPO = acceptable data loss.
DR sites: hot (minutes, costly), warm (hours-days), cold (days-weeks, cheap).
Testing: checklist, tabletop, simulation, parallel, full interruption – at least yearly.
Conclusion: Tested DR reduces downtime and data loss.

Long Q5. Explain Business Continuity Planning – BIA, strategies, lifecycle, roles – and differentiate BCP and DRP.

Definition: BCP is a plan to continue critical business functions during and after a disruption.
Objectives: safety of people, continue critical services, reduce loss, comply with regulators.
BIA: identifies critical processes and impact over time; sets MTD, RTO, RPO and priorities. Risk assessment finds threats like flood, cyber attack, pandemic.
Strategies: alternate site, work from home, redundant IT/power, manual workaround, alternate suppliers, insurance.
Emergency response and communication: protect life, assess, activate crisis team, contain, continue; call tree, one spokesperson.
Lifecycle: Initiate → Risk assessment → BIA → Strategy → Develop plan → Train → Test → Maintain.
Roles: top management, BCP coordinator, business heads, IT/DR team, emergency team, communication team, HR, employees.
BCP vs DRP: BCP = whole business; DRP = IT recovery; DRP is part of BCP.
Example: bank shifts to DR site and staff work from home during a flood.

Long Q6. Explain security audit procedures and policy development with a practical example of a college lab.

Audit: definition, objectives; types – internal/external, technical/administrative; process – planning, information gathering, risk identification, control evaluation, execution, report, corrective action and follow-up. Lab example: scope 40 PCs; findings – shared admin password (high), cracked software (high), outdated antivirus (medium); actions and re-check after one month.
Policy: definition; types – organizational, issue-specific (AUP, password), system-specific; lifecycle – draft, review, approve, communicate, implement, enforce, review; common mistakes.
Link: policies define the rules; audits check whether the rules are followed.

G. ✍️ Model answers – how to write for marks

Formula for a 5–8 mark answer: Definition (1–2 lines) → Points/Steps (5–7 bullets, bold keywords) → Diagram/flowchart or table → Example (1–2 lines) → Conclusion (1 line). Use headings, underline keywords and write neat diagrams.

Model answer – “Explain the digital signature” (5 marks)

Definition: A digital signature is an electronic signature created using asymmetric cryptography to prove the identity of the sender and the integrity of the message.
Working: 1) Sender creates a hash of the message. 2) Hash is encrypted with the sender’s private key – this is the signature. 3) Message, signature and certificate are sent. 4) Receiver decrypts the signature with the sender’s public key and compares it with a new hash. 5) If equal, the message is genuine.
Features: authentication, integrity, non-repudiation. Legal basis: IT Act, 2000 (sections 3A, 5; certificates by licensed Certifying Authorities under CCA).
Example: filing company returns with a DSC token.

Model answer – “Write short note on Section 66A” (3 marks)

Section 66A of the IT Act (inserted in 2008) punished sending grossly offensive, menacing or false messages through a computer or communication device. It was vague and misused. On 24 March 2015 the Supreme Court, in Shreya Singhal v. Union of India, struck it down completely as unconstitutional under Article 19(1)(a). So it is no longer in force.

Model answer – “Why is a DR plan important for a company?” (3 marks)

A DR plan helps a company (1) restore critical systems quickly and reduce downtime, (2) limit data loss using backups, (3) protect customer trust and reputation, (4) meet legal and contractual duties, and (5) give staff clear instructions in an emergency. Example: restoring the server from backup after a disk crash.

2. 📘 Chapter-wise summary

  1. Security standards give minimum, agreed rules for protecting information and allow audit and certification. Policies say what, standards say how much, procedures say how.
  2. ISO 27001 sets requirements for an ISMS using risk management and 93 Annex A controls. Organizations get certified after internal audit, management review and external audits.
  3. IT Act, 2000 recognises e-records and digital signatures and punishes cyber offences. The 2008 amendment added many sections; section 66A was struck down in 2015.
  4. IPR laws protect creations of the mind – copyright, patent, trademark, designs, GI, trade secrets. Software piracy is punishable under the Copyright Act.
  5. Security audit is a planned, evidence-based review of controls leading to a report and follow-up.
  6. Security policies are management-approved rules that must be developed, approved, communicated, enforced and reviewed.
  7. Disaster recovery restores IT after disaster using backups, DR sites and tested plans guided by RTO and RPO.
  8. BCP keeps critical business functions running using BIA, strategies and roles; DRP is its IT part.
